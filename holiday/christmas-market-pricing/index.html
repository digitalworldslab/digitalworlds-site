How to Price Handmade Items for a Christmas Market (With a Worked Example)

Christmas markets are the busiest weekends of the year for many makers, and also the easiest place to undercharge. Here is a pricing formula that pays you for your time, with a worked example.

The formula

Price = materials + packaging + (minutes × hourly rate ÷ 60) + overhead share + profit

Then check: does the price still leave a profit after the market stall fee, card reader fee or Etsy fees?

Worked example: a soy candle

CostAmount
Wax, wick, fragrance, jar€4.10
Label and gift box€0.90
Time: 15 minutes at €18/hour€4.50
Overheads (stall fee, tools, heating) per candle€1.00
Cost to make€10.50
Profit (30%)€3.15
Retail price, rounded€14.00

If a shop asks for wholesale, the usual rule is about half of retail. At €7.00 this candle would lose money, so either raise retail or lower the cost to make before saying yes.

Five mistakes that cost makers money

  • Not paying yourself. Your time is a cost, even if you enjoy the work.
  • Forgetting packaging. Gift boxes, ribbons and bags add up quickly at Christmas.
  • Ignoring the stall fee. Divide it across the number of items you expect to sell.
  • Card fees. Card readers usually take a small percentage of each sale.
  • Same price everywhere without checking. Online platforms take their own fees, so check your profit per channel.

Questions

What is a simple pricing formula for handmade items?

Materials + your time at an hourly rate + a share of overheads, then add profit. Check that the price still works after market or platform fees.

Should market prices match my Etsy prices?

Keep them close. Etsy takes listing, transaction and payment fees, so check your profit there separately.

What if my price feels too high?

Make the item faster, buy materials in bulk, or make a smaller version at a lower price point. Cutting your hourly rate to zero is not a plan.
