50/30/20 Budget Rule: A Simple Example With Real Numbers

The 50/30/20 rule is the easiest budget to start with: three buckets, three percentages, no categories to argue about.

Example: €2,800 a month take-home

BucketSharePer monthTypical items
Needs50%1,400Rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, transport, minimum debt payments
Wants30%840Eating out, streaming, hobbies, holidays, shopping
Savings & debt20%560Emergency fund, sinking funds, extra debt payments, investing

How to set it up in 15 minutes

  1. Write down your take-home pay for the month.
  2. List last month's spending and tag each line need, want or saving.
  3. Compare your real split with 50/30/20. Most people find wants are bigger than they thought.
  4. Move money to savings on payday, before you spend it.

Questions

What is the 50/30/20 rule?

Split your take-home pay: 50% for needs (rent, bills, groceries, minimum debt payments), 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and extra debt payments.

Is it based on gross or net income?

Use take-home pay, the amount that actually lands in your account after tax.

What if my needs are more than 50%?

That's common where rent is high. Start from your real numbers, keep savings at least small but regular, and trim wants first.
