How to Calculate Your Net Worth (Worked Example + What to Count)

One number that shows whether you're actually getting richer, with a worked example.

Net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. One number, and the trend over time tells you more about your finances than any single month's budget.

Worked example

AssetsValue
Current account2,400
Savings6,000
Pension18,500
Car (resale value)7,000
Total assets33,900
DebtsOwed
Car loan4,200
Credit card1,300
Student loan9,800
Total debts15,300

Net worth = 33,900 − 15,300 = 18,600. Debt-to-asset ratio: 15,300 ÷ 33,900 = 45%.

What to count (and what to leave out)

  • Count: cash, savings, investments, pensions, your home and car at a realistic resale value.
  • Leave out: furniture, clothes and gadgets. They're hard to value and rarely sell for much.
  • Debts: mortgage, loans, credit cards, anything you owe, at today's balance.

Track it monthly, not daily

Update the numbers once a month, on the same day. The change versus last month is the number to watch: paying down debt and saving both push it up. A negative net worth is common early on, especially with student loans; the direction matters more than the starting point.

Questions

How do you calculate net worth?

Add up the value of everything you own, then subtract everything you owe. In the example, 33,900 − 15,300 = 18,600.

Should I include my house and car?

Yes, at a realistic resale value, and include the mortgage or car loan as a debt.

Is a negative net worth bad?

It is common early on, especially with student loans. What matters is that it rises month by month.
