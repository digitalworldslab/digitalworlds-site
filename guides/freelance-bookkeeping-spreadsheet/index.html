Freelance Bookkeeping Without an Accountant: What to Track Each Month

Invoices, expenses, VAT and tax set-aside in one place, with a worked example.

You don't need accounting software to keep freelance books in order. You need four things in one place: invoices, expenses, VAT, and a number for how much to put aside.

Use your own country's VAT rate and your own set-aside rate. If you're not VAT-registered, skip the VAT columns. The habit is the point: move the VAT and tax set-aside to a separate account the day a client pays.

What to track

  • Invoices: number, client, net, VAT, gross, due date, and whether it's paid. Overdue invoices are the most common freelance cash problem.
  • Expenses: with the VAT you can reclaim and a tick for "receipt saved".
  • VAT summary each quarter: VAT charged minus VAT reclaimable.
  • Mileage, if you drive for work, with your own rate per km.

Questions

What records should a freelancer keep?

Invoices sent and paid, business expenses with receipts, VAT charged and reclaimable if you are registered, and mileage if you drive for work.

How much should a freelancer set aside for tax?

It depends on your country and income. Many freelancers set aside a fixed percentage of profit, such as 25 to 30%, and adjust once they know their real rate.

Do I need accounting software?

Not to start. A spreadsheet that tracks invoices, expenses and VAT covers most small freelancers; an accountant can still do the annual return.
