How Much Should Your Emergency Fund Be? Find Your Number in 5 Steps

Not a vague "3 to 6 months": a number worked out from your own essential bills.

The usual advice is "3 to 6 months of expenses". The useful part is which expenses: only the essentials you would still pay if your income stopped tomorrow.

Step 1: add up your essential monthly costs

EssentialPer month
Rent900
Utilities140
Groceries350
Transport120
Insurance60
Phone40
Minimum debt payments150
Essential total1,760

Leave out eating out, subscriptions and holidays. In an emergency those stop first.

Step 2: choose how many months

CoverTargetWho it often suits
Starter1,000Anyone with no savings yet: get here first
3 months5,280Stable job, two incomes in the household
6 months10,560Single income, self-employed, or irregular pay

Step 3: work out how long it will take

Saving €250 a month, the €1,000 starter takes 4 months and 3 months of cover (5,280) takes 22 months. That feels slow, which is why milestones help: celebrate 1,000, then one month, then three.

Step 4: keep it separate and only use it for emergencies

A separate savings account you don't see every day works best. Car repairs and Christmas are not emergencies; they belong in sinking funds.

Step 5: refill after you use it

When you take money out, pause other extra savings and top it back up first.

Questions

How much should an emergency fund be?

A common target is 3 to 6 months of essential expenses. Start with a 1,000 starter fund, then build to full cover.

Should I pay off debt or build an emergency fund first?

Many people build a small starter fund first so a surprise bill doesn't go on a card, then focus on debt, then finish the full fund.

Where should I keep my emergency fund?

In a separate, easy-access savings account that you don't use for day-to-day spending.
