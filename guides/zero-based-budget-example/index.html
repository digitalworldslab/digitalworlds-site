Zero-Based Budget Example: Give Every Euro a Job (€2,600 Worked Example)

Every euro gets a job before the month starts. Here is a full example with real-looking numbers you can copy.

A zero-based budget means income minus everything you assign equals zero. Not zero in your account: zero unassigned. Savings and debt payments count as jobs too.

How to build one in 4 steps

  1. Start with the money you actually have this month, not what you hope to earn.
  2. Fixed bills first: rent, utilities, insurance, minimum debt payments.
  3. Then the irregular stuff: put a monthly amount into sinking funds for car repairs, Christmas and holidays, so those costs stop being surprises. See the sinking funds list.
  4. Assign what's left to savings, extra debt payments and fun money until "to be assigned" hits 0.
What if you overspend an envelope? Move money from another envelope and keep the total at zero. That's the whole trick: you decide what gives way, instead of finding out at the end of the month.

Zero-based vs 50/30/20

The 50/30/20 rule is faster to start: three buckets, no categories. Zero-based takes 20 minutes a month but shows exactly where every euro goes. Many people start with 50/30/20 and switch once they want more control.

Questions

What is a zero-based budget?

A budget where income minus everything you assign equals zero. Every euro has a job, including savings and extra debt payments.

Does zero-based mean I spend everything?

No. Savings, sinking funds and extra debt payments are jobs too. Zero means nothing is left unassigned.

What if my income changes every month?

Budget only the money you already have. When more comes in, assign it then.
