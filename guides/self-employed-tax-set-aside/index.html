How Much to Set Aside for Taxes When Self-Employed (US, 2026)

When nobody withholds tax from your pay, you have to do it yourself. The easiest way is a fixed percentage moved to a separate account every time you get paid.

What you owe, in two parts

  • Self-employment tax: 15.3% on 92.35% of your net earnings (Social Security 12.4% up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, Medicare 2.9% on all of it).
  • Income tax: federal, and state where it applies, based on your bracket.

Add your income tax on top. That's why many freelancers set aside 25–30% of every payment, then adjust once their real numbers are in.

A simple system: every time a client pays, move your set-aside percentage to a separate savings account. Pay your quarterly estimate from that account. Keep receipts as you go, not in April.

Questions

How much should I set aside for taxes if I'm self-employed?

A common rule of thumb is 25–30% of profit, but it depends on your income and state. Self-employment tax alone is 15.3% on 92.35% of your net earnings, before income tax.

What is the self-employment tax rate in 2026?

15.3%: 12.4% Social Security on earnings up to the 2026 wage base of $184,500, plus 2.9% Medicare on all earnings (with an extra 0.9% above higher-income thresholds).

When are quarterly estimated taxes due?

Usually mid-April, mid-June, mid-September and mid-January. Check the IRS calendar for the exact dates each year.
