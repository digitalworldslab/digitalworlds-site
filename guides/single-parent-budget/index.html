Single Parent Budget: Worked Example + Free Calculator

Budgeting on one income with children means more fixed costs and less room for surprises. This page shows a realistic example and a calculator that includes support payments, benefits and the costs that are easy to forget, like school trips and growth spurts.

Tips that make the biggest difference

  • Budget support payments separately. If they arrive late or not at all, the bills should still be covered by pay and benefits.
  • Check every entitlement once a year. Childcare subsidies, tax credits and school meal schemes change, and many families don't claim what they could.
  • Use sinking funds for the predictable spikes. School uniforms in August, birthdays, Christmas. See the sinking funds list.
  • Build a one-month buffer first. With one income, a broken washing machine is an emergency. Even €20 a week adds up to over €1,000 a year.
  • Give the kids a small envelope. Cash envelopes for pocket money and treats teach them budgeting too (cash stuffing guide).

Questions

How do single parents budget on one income?

List every income source separately (pay, support, benefits), cover the fixed costs from the most reliable ones, plan per-child costs monthly and build a small buffer before anything else.

Should child support be counted as income in a budget?

Yes, but budget so essential bills are covered even if a support payment is late.

How much does a child cost per month?

It varies hugely by age and country. Beyond food and childcare, plan roughly 30 to 60 a month per child for clothes, school costs and activities.

What is the first savings goal for a single parent?

One month of essential costs in an emergency fund, then work up to three months.
