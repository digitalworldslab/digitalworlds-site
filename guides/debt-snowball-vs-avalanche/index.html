Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Which Pays Off Debt Faster? (Worked Example)

Both methods use the same idea: pay the minimum on everything, throw every extra euro at one debt, and roll the payment forward when it's cleared. The only difference is which debt you target first.

The example

Four debts, €315 a month in minimum payments, plus €150 a month extra (€465 in total, every month):

DebtBalanceInterest (APR)Minimum
Store card65026%25
Medical bill9000%50
Credit card2,40022%60
Car loan7,5008%180

The result

Snowball (smallest first)Avalanche (highest rate first)
Debt-free in28 months28 months
Total interest≈ 1,466≈ 1,384
Payoff order (month)Store card (4), Medical bill (8), Credit card (16), Car loan (28)Store card (4), Credit card (15), Medical bill (18), Car loan (28)

Without the extra €150 (still rolling freed-up minimums forward) the same debts take 46 months and about 2,829 in interest. The extra payment matters far more than the method.

Which should you pick? If the interest gap is small, as here, pick the one you'll keep doing. Early wins (the snowball) keep many people going. If one debt has a much higher rate than the rest, the avalanche saves real money.

Questions

Is the debt snowball or avalanche better?

The avalanche usually costs less interest because it targets the highest rate first. The snowball pays off small balances sooner, which many people find easier to stick with. In our example the gap was €82 over 28 months.

What is the debt snowball method?

List your debts from smallest to largest balance, pay the minimum on all of them, and put every extra euro on the smallest. When it's gone, roll its payment into the next one.

What is the debt avalanche method?

The same rolling payment, but you target the debt with the highest interest rate first instead of the smallest balance.
