Wedding Budget Breakdown by Category: Percentages + a €15,000 Example

A starting split by category, a worked example, and the habits that stop the total creeping up.

Most wedding budgets go wrong in two places: the venue and catering line is bigger than expected, and there's no money left for the small things that add up at the end. A starting split by category fixes both.

Example: €15,000 wedding, 80 guests

That works out at €187.50 per guest. These percentages are a common starting point, not a rule. If the venue quote comes in at 55%, take the difference from another line straight away rather than hoping it evens out.

4 habits that keep a wedding on budget

  1. Fix the total first, then the guest count. Guest count drives venue and catering, the biggest line.
  2. Keep a 10% buffer for alterations, extra hours, tips and last-minute decor.
  3. Track deposits and balances, not just prices. Most vendors take a deposit now and the balance a few weeks before the day.
  4. Write down who pays what. If parents are contributing, note what was promised and what has arrived.

Questions

What percentage of a wedding budget goes to the venue?

Venue and catering together often take around 40 to 50% of the total, which is why guest count matters so much.

How much buffer should a wedding budget have?

Around 10% of the total is a common choice. It covers alterations, overtime, tips and last-minute extras.

How do I work out cost per guest?

Divide the total budget by the number of guests. For €15,000 and 80 guests it is €187.50.
