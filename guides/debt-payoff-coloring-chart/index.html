Debt Payoff Colouring Chart: How the 100-Square Method Works

Turn one big scary number into 100 small squares you can colour in.

A debt payoff colouring chart turns a scary total into 100 small squares. Each square is 1% of what you owe. Every time you pay off another 1%, you colour one in.

Example: €6,400 of debt

Amount
Total debt6,400
One square (1%)64
Monthly payment toward debt400
Squares coloured per month6.25
Months to colour all 10016

That's it: total ÷ 100 = the value of one square. Some months you'll colour six squares, some months seven. The chart makes progress visible long before the balance looks small.

Why it works

  • Small wins: one square is €64, not €6,400. You get a win every few days of saving.
  • Visible: stick a printed chart on the fridge and everyone in the house sees it.
  • Honest: you colour from real payments, so the chart can't run ahead of your bank balance.

One chart or one per debt?

Use one chart for the total if you want a single finish line. Use one chart per debt if you're following the snowball or avalanche method, so you can watch the first debt disappear completely.

Questions

How does a debt colouring chart work?

Divide your total debt by 100. Each time you pay off that amount, colour one square.

Should I use one chart or one per debt?

One chart gives a single finish line; one per debt works well with the snowball or avalanche method.

Does colouring in actually help?

Many people find visible progress keeps them going through the long middle part of paying off debt.
