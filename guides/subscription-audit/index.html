The 20-minute subscription audit

  1. Search your bank statement for the last 3 months, not just one: quarterly and yearly charges hide otherwise.
  2. Check the app stores. Apple and Google list every active subscription in account settings.
  3. Search your email for "receipt", "renewal" and "your plan".
  4. Sort into keep, pause, cancel. Ask: did I use it in the last 30 days? Would I sign up again today at this price?
  5. Rotate instead of stacking. Keep one streaming service at a time and switch every month or two.
  6. Put renewal dates in your calendar for the yearly ones, a week before they charge.

Where the money usually hides

  • Free trials that rolled into paid plans.
  • Two services doing the same job (two cloud storage plans, two music apps).
  • Family plans still paying for someone who moved out.
  • Price rises: most services raised prices in the last two years, quietly.

Put the money you free up somewhere visible: an emergency fund or an extra debt payment in the debt snowball calculator.

Questions

How do I find all my subscriptions?

Check 3 months of bank and card statements, the subscription lists in the Apple and Google app stores, and your email for receipts and renewal notices.

How much does the average person spend on subscriptions?

Surveys usually land between 50 and 100 a month per household, and most people underestimate their own total. The calculator above gives your real figure.

Is it better to pay yearly or monthly?

Yearly is cheaper only for services you are sure to use all year. For everything else monthly keeps it easy to cancel.

What is subscription rotation?

Keeping one streaming service at a time and switching to another after you finish what you wanted to watch.
