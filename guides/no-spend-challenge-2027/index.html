No-spend challenge rules that actually work

  1. Write your essentials list first. Rent, bills, groceries from a list, fuel or a travel pass, medicine, childcare. Anything not on the list is a no.
  2. Decide the grey areas in advance. Birthday gifts, a work lunch, a broken phone charger. Decide now, not at the till.
  3. Remove the easy triggers. Delete saved cards from shopping apps, unsubscribe from sale emails and turn off app notifications.
  4. Use what you have. January is a good month for a "pantry challenge": plan meals around what is already in the cupboard and freezer.
  5. Move the savings on day 31. Transfer the difference to savings or a debt payment so it doesn't quietly get spent in February.

Free things to do instead of shopping

  • Library books, films and audiobooks.
  • Walks, parks, free museum days and local events.
  • Cooking a "fridge clean-out" dinner with friends, everyone brings what they have.
  • Selling things you no longer use; it often pays for February's fun money.

Questions

What is a no-spend challenge?

A set period, such as a weekend, a month or a year, when you only spend on essentials you agreed in advance, like rent, bills and groceries.

Do groceries count in a no-spend challenge?

Groceries from a list count as essential. Snacks, takeaway and extra treats do not.

How much can you save in a no-spend month?

It depends on your normal non-essential spending. Many households spend 150 to 400 a month on eating out, shopping and small purchases, so that is the usual range.

What if I break the challenge?

Mark the day, note what triggered it and carry on. A month with 25 no-spend days still beats a normal month.
