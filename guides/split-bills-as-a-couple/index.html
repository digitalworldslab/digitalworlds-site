How to Split Bills as a Couple: 50/50 vs By Income (Worked Example)

50/50 or by income? A worked example, and a simple system that ends the "who owes who" conversation.

Most couples split shared bills one of two ways: 50/50, or in proportion to income. Neither is "right". The fair one is the one you both agree to and can see clearly each month.

Example: €2,000 of shared costs

Alex takes home €3,000 a month and Sam €2,000. Shared costs (rent, utilities, groceries, internet) come to €2,000.

With 50/50, Sam keeps half as much as Alex. Splitting by income leaves both with the same share of their pay: 40% of each income goes to shared costs.

How to set it up

  1. List the shared costs once: everything you both use.
  2. Pick the split: 50/50, by income, or a custom % you agree on.
  3. Log who paid what during the month, from either account.
  4. Settle up once a month with one transfer, instead of paying each other back for every shop.
  5. Keep personal money personal. Each of you gets spending money that nobody has to justify.
Joint account or not? A joint account for shared bills works well when you split by percentage: each partner pays their share in on payday and the bills go out from there. If you'd rather keep separate accounts, the monthly settle-up does the same job.

Questions

Is it fair to split bills 50/50?

It is simple, but if one partner earns much more, 50/50 leaves the lower earner with far less left over. Splitting by income keeps the share of each pay the same.

How do you split bills by income?

Add both take-home pays, work out each person's percentage of the total, and pay that percentage of shared costs.

How often should couples settle up?

Once a month is enough for most couples: one number, one transfer.
