Credit Card Minimum Payments: Why They Take 9 Years (and How to Pay Off Faster)

A worked example shows how much a shrinking minimum payment really costs, and the simple fix.

Credit card minimum payments shrink as your balance shrinks. That feels kind, but it's why paying only the minimum can take close to ten years.

Example: €3,000 at 22% APR

Assume the minimum is 2% of the balance plus that month's interest, with a €25 floor. The first minimum is €115.

PlanMonths to clearInterest paid
Minimum only (shrinks over time)112 (9 yrs 4 mo)≈ 2,395
Fixed €115 every month36≈ 1,118
Fixed €150 every month26≈ 771
Fixed €200 every month18≈ 541

Simply keeping the payment at the first minimum (€115) instead of letting it shrink saves around €1,277 and more than 6 years. These are estimates; your card's exact minimum rule and interest method will change the numbers a little.

4 ways out of the minimum payment trap

  1. Fix your payment at today's minimum or higher, and don't let it drop.
  2. Pick a date and work backwards to the payment you need to be clear by then.
  3. Pay more than once a month if your card allows it, to cut the balance interest is charged on.
  4. Stop adding to the card while you pay it off. Use a debit card for daily spending.

Questions

How long does it take to pay off a credit card with minimum payments?

With a minimum of 2% of the balance plus interest, €3,000 at 22% APR takes around 112 months, over 9 years, in our example.

What is the fastest way to pay off a credit card?

Fix your payment instead of following the shrinking minimum, pay as much above it as you can, and stop adding new spending to the card.

Does paying more than the minimum help?

Yes. In the example, a fixed €150 a month clears the card in 26 months and saves around €1,600 of interest compared with minimum-only.
